\doxysubsection{Detailed Description}
a spherical Gaussian interpolation kernel 

\doxylink{classvtk_gaussian_kernel}{vtk\+Gaussian\+Kernel} is an interpolation kernel that simply returns the weights for all points found in the sphere defined by radius R. \doxylink{requirements_The}{The} weights are computed as:\+ exp(-\/(s\texorpdfstring{$\ast$}{*}r/\+R)\texorpdfstring{$^\wedge$}{\string^}2) where r is the distance from the point to be interpolated to a neighboring point within R. \doxylink{requirements_The}{The} sharpness s simply affects the rate of fall off of the Gaussian. (A more general Gaussian kernel is available from \doxylink{classvtk_ellipsoidal_gaussian_kernel}{vtk\+Ellipsoidal\+Gaussian\+Kernel}.)

\begin{DoxyWarning}{Warning}
\doxylink{requirements_The}{The} weights are normalized sp that SUM(\+Wi) = 1. If a neighbor point p precisely lies on the point to be interpolated, then the interpolated point takes on the values associated with p.
\end{DoxyWarning}

Given a point x, a list of basis points p\+Ids, and a probability weighting function prob, compute interpolation weights associated with these basis points. Note that basis points list p\+Ids, the probability weighting prob, and the weights array are provided by the caller of the method, and may be dynamically resized as necessary. \doxylink{requirements_The}{The} method returns the number of weights (p\+Ids may be resized in some cases). Typically this method is called after \doxylink{classvtk_generalized_kernel_a3c82d34597e534268c950babf870fbab}{Compute\+Basis()}, although advanced users can invoke \doxylink{classvtk_gaussian_kernel_a7dee0c8c2358e93ab76ee467d2566e70}{Compute\+Weights()} and provide the interpolation basis points p\+Ids directly. \doxylink{requirements_The}{The} probably weighting prob are numbers 0\texorpdfstring{$<$}{<}=prob\texorpdfstring{$<$}{<}=1 which are multiplied against the interpolation weights before normalization. They are estimates of local confidence of weights. \doxylink{requirements_The}{The} prob may be nullptr in which all probabilities are considered =1.
